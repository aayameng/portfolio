Step 1 of 9 · The domain

A plane cuts the three axes

The plane meets the axes one unit from the origin, at \((1,0,0)\), \((0,1,0)\) and \((0,0,1)\). With the three coordinate planes it fences in a tetrahedron.

$$x+y+z=1$$

Everything in the first octant below the plane is the solid we will weigh.

Step 2 of 9 · The density

Density under the plane

Each point of the solid \(V\) carries a density \(\rho=x^{l-1}y^{m-1}z^{n-1}\). The picture uses \(l=m=n=2\), so \(\rho=xyz\): dots crowd together and turn gold where the solid is densest, and thin out toward the faces.

$$I=\iiint_V x^{l-1}y^{m-1}z^{n-1}\,dx\,dy\,dz,\qquad V:\ x,y,z\ge 0,\ x+y+z\le 1$$

The integral \(I\) adds up all that density: the total mass of \(V\).

Step 3 of 9 · The bounds

Three nested sweeps

The white needle rises in \(z\) from the floor to the plane. The teal sheet is the slice at one value of \(x\), swept by needles in \(y\). Sliding the sheet along \(x\) fills the solid.

$$I=\int_0^1 x^{l-1}\!\left(\int_0^{1-x} y^{m-1}\!\left(\int_0^{1-x-y} z^{n-1}\,dz\right)dy\right)dx$$

So \(0\le z\le 1-x-y\), then \(0\le y\le 1-x\), then \(0\le x\le 1\).

Step 4 of 9 · Inner integral

The needle: integrate in z

Fix \(x\) and \(y\). The needle climbs from \(z=0\) to the plane at \(z=1-x-y\), collecting \(z^{n-1}\) on the way.

$$\int_0^{1-x-y} z^{n-1}\,dz=\left[\frac{z^n}{n}\right]_0^{1-x-y}=\frac{(1-x-y)^n}{n}$$

Each needle now weighs one number that depends only on \(x\) and \(y\):

$$I=\frac1n\int_0^1 x^{l-1}\int_0^{1-x} y^{m-1}(1-x-y)^n\,dy\,dx$$

Step 5 of 9 · Middle integral

The sheet: integrate in y

Freeze \(x\). Needles sweep the sheet from \(y=0\) to \(y=1-x\). Substitute \(y=(1-x)u\) so the limits become \(0\) to \(1\):

$$\int_0^{1-x} y^{m-1}(1-x-y)^n\,dy=(1-x)^{m+n}\int_0^1 u^{m-1}(1-u)^n\,du=(1-x)^{m+n}B(m,n+1)$$

Since \(\Gamma(n+1)=n\,\Gamma(n)\), the \(\tfrac1n\) from the last step cancels:

$$\frac1n B(m,n+1)=\frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n+1)}{n\,\Gamma(m+n+1)}=\frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m+n+1)}$$

Step 6 of 9 · Outer integral

The slab: integrate in x

Slide the sheet from \(x=0\) to \(x=1\). The bright dots are the mass collected so far. One more Beta integral remains:

$$I=\frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m+n+1)}\int_0^1 x^{l-1}(1-x)^{m+n}\,dx=\frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m+n+1)}\,B(l,\,m+n+1)$$

Step 7 of 9 · Result

The total mass

Write the last Beta function in Gamma form. The factors \(\Gamma(m+n+1)\) cancel:

$$I=\frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m+n+1)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(l)\Gamma(m+n+1)}{\Gamma(l+m+n+1)}$$
$$I=\frac{\Gamma(l)\,\Gamma(m)\,\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(l+m+n+1)}$$

For the pictured density, \(l=m=n=2\): \(I=\dfrac{1\cdot1\cdot1}{\Gamma(7)}=\dfrac{1}{720}\).

Step 8 of 9 · Why factorials?

Where the factorials come from

Take the plainest case: density 1, so \(l=m=n=1\). The sheet sweeping across the solid is the same one as before. Each integration raises the power by one and divides by the new power:

$$\begin{aligned}\int_0^{1-x-y}dz&=\frac{1-x-y}{1}\\ \int_0^{1-x}\frac{1-x-y}{1}\,dy&=\frac{(1-x)^2}{1\cdot 2}\\ \int_0^{1}\frac{(1-x)^2}{1\cdot 2}\,dx&=\frac{1}{1\cdot 2\cdot 3}=\frac{1}{3!}\end{aligned}$$

That is the tetrahedron’s volume, \(\tfrac16\). Every integration piles one more factor onto the denominator, and that pile is a factorial. With the density \(x^{l-1}y^{m-1}z^{n-1}\) the powers start higher, and for whole numbers the same staircase gives:

$$I=\frac{(l-1)!\,(m-1)!\,(n-1)!}{(l+m+n)!}$$

The Gamma function is this factorial extended to any real exponent. The rule \(\Gamma(s+1)=s\,\Gamma(s)\) says “divide by the new power”, and \(\Gamma(k+1)=k!\). Swap each factorial for a Gamma and you get the formula from the last step.

Step 9 of 9 · Factorials only

The full derivation, no Gamma

For whole numbers \(l,m,n\) the Gamma function isn’t needed. One lemma does the heavy lifting: integrate by parts once, moving a power from \((1-u)\) onto \(u\).

$$\int_0^1 u^{a}(1-u)^{b}\,du=\frac{b}{a+1}\int_0^1 u^{a+1}(1-u)^{b-1}\,du$$

Repeat \(b\) times, until no \((1-u)\) is left:

$$\int_0^1 u^{a}(1-u)^{b}\,du=\frac{b!}{(a+1)(a+2)\cdots(a+b)}\cdot\frac{1}{a+b+1}=\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b+1)!}\qquad(\ast)$$

z: the needle, as before.

$$\int_0^{1-x-y}z^{n-1}\,dz=\frac{(1-x-y)^n}{n}$$

y: substitute \(y=(1-x)u\), then use \((\ast)\) with \(a=m-1,\ b=n\). Since \(n!/n=(n-1)!\):

$$\frac1n\int_0^{1-x}y^{m-1}(1-x-y)^n\,dy=\frac{(1-x)^{m+n}}{n}\cdot\frac{(m-1)!\,n!}{(m+n)!}=(1-x)^{m+n}\,\frac{(m-1)!\,(n-1)!}{(m+n)!}$$

x: use \((\ast)\) again, with \(a=l-1,\ b=m+n\):

$$I=\frac{(m-1)!\,(n-1)!}{(m+n)!}\int_0^1 x^{l-1}(1-x)^{m+n}\,dx=\frac{(m-1)!\,(n-1)!}{(m+n)!}\cdot\frac{(l-1)!\,(m+n)!}{(l+m+n)!}$$

The \((m+n)!\) cancels:

$$I=\frac{(l-1)!\,(m-1)!\,(n-1)!}{(l+m+n)!}$$

Check: \(l=m=n=2\) gives \(\dfrac{1\cdot1\cdot1}{6!}=\dfrac{1}{720}\), matching the picture.
