Why is the wire shorter in the moving-charge frame?

Ride with q⁺. The charge is at rest, the infinite wire moves to +x′, and both endpoints must be measured at one shared S′ time.

q⁺ at rest · wire moves +x′
01
Look at the wire in S′Spacetime view · moving ions trace tilted worldlines
Cu⁺ worldlines · wire moves +x′ one shared instant t′ = 0 L₀ reference from S

Read the gap horizontally where the cyan slice crosses both gold worldlines. Those events share one S′ time, so this is a valid length measurement. Only the distance parallel to the motion (x′) contracts; transverse dimensions do not.

\((c\Delta t)^2=(v_q\Delta t)^2+(c\Delta\tau)^2\)

Measure the ion gap on that sliceSame t′ is the essential condition for length
Lorentz transformation
x′ = γq(x + vqt), t′ = γq(t + vqx/c²)
Choose simultaneous endpoints
Δt′ = 0 ⇒ Δt = −vqΔx/c²
Substitute and simplify
Δx′ = γq(Δx + vqΔt) = Δx/γq = L₀/γq
Measured Cu⁺ spacing in S′0.816 u8.35% shorter than L₀

Why the electrons contract even moreTheir worldlines are already moving in S

The S′ slice reaches the second electron at an earlier lab time. During that time difference, the electron has also drifted to the right in S. That changes the lab-frame separation of the two selected events before the Lorentz contraction is applied.

Correct the lab separation
Δx = d₀ + vdΔt ⇒ Δx = d₀/(1 + βqβd)
Transform that separation to S′
