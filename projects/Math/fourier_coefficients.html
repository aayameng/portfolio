Derivation of the Fourier Series Coefficients

Consider a piecewise smooth periodic function $f(x)$ with period $2L$ defined on the interval $[-L, L]$. Its trigonometric Fourier series representation is written as:

$$f(x) = a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left[ a_n \cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) + b_n \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) \right]$$

1. Orthogonality of Trigonometric Functions

For any positive integers $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$, integrating products of harmonics over a full period $[-L, L]$ yields the orthogonality relations:

$$\int_{-L}^{L} \cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx = 0, \qquad \int_{-L}^{L} \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx = 0$$ $$\int_{-L}^{L} \cos\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right)\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx = 0 \quad \forall\, m, n$$ $$\int_{-L}^{L} \cos\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right)\cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx = \int_{-L}^{L} \sin\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right)\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } m \neq n \\ L & \text{if } m = n \end{cases}$$

2. Derivation of $a_0$ (Mean Value)

Integrate both sides of the Fourier series across $[-L, L]$, assuming term-by-term integration is valid:

$$\int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\,dx = \int_{-L}^{L} a_0\,dx + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left[ a_n \underbrace{\int_{-L}^{L} \cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx}_{0} + b_n \underbrace{\int_{-L}^{L} \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx}_{0} \right]$$ $$\int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\,dx = 2L\,a_0 \implies \boxed{a_0 = \frac{1}{2L} \int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\,dx}$$

3. Derivation of $a_m$ (Cosine Coefficients)

Multiply both sides by $\cos\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right)$ for a fixed integer $m \ge 1$ and integrate from $-L$ to $L$:

$$\begin{aligned} \int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\cos\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right) dx &= a_0 \underbrace{\int_{-L}^{L} \cos\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right) dx}_{0} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \underbrace{\int_{-L}^{L} \cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\cos\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right) dx}_{L\,\delta_{nm}} \\ &\quad + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n \underbrace{\int_{-L}^{L} \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\cos\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right) dx}_{0} \end{aligned}$$ $$\int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\cos\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right) dx = a_m L \implies \boxed{a_n = \frac{1}{L} \int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx}$$

4. Derivation of $b_m$ (Sine Coefficients)

Similarly, multiply both sides by $\sin\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right)$ for $m \ge 1$ and integrate over $[-L, L]$:

$$\begin{aligned} \int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\sin\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right) dx &= a_0 \underbrace{\int_{-L}^{L} \sin\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right) dx}_{0} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \underbrace{\int_{-L}^{L} \cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\sin\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right) dx}_{0} \\ &\quad + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n \underbrace{\int_{-L}^{L} \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\sin\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right) dx}_{L\,\delta_{nm}} \end{aligned}$$ $$\int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\sin\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right) dx = b_m L \implies \boxed{b_n = \frac{1}{L} \int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx}$$
