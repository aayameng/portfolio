Amber is the triangle OPP′ of the N-gon. Push the midpoint of its side out to the circle at Q and the cream sliver appears, making a kite. Triangle and kite share the base c = PP′, but the triangle’s height is h = cos(π/N) and the kite’s is 1. That one ratio, repeated for every triangle, is a factor of the formula.

One identity, \(\sin 2x=2\sin x\cos x\), proved from a picture and then applied forever. Drag any angle slider: every figure with one follows it.

0The unit circle and one area rule

A radius at angle \(x\) and its perpendicular to the axis form a right triangle with hypotenuse \(1\). By definition its legs are \(\cos x\) and \(\sin x\), and Pythagoras gives \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\).

Area rule. Two sides \(a,b\) enclosing an angle \(\gamma\) make a triangle of base \(a\) and height \(b\sin\gamma\), so \[\text{Area}=\tfrac12\,ab\sin\gamma .\]

1Geometric proof that sin 2x = 2 sin x cos x

Take \(OA=OB=1\) with \(\angle AOB=2x\). Count the area of this one triangle twice.

Left, two sides and the angle between. The area rule gives \(\tfrac12\cdot1\cdot1\cdot\sin 2x\).

Right, base times height. The altitude \(OM\) of an isosceles triangle is also its bisector and median, so \(\angle AOM=x\). Right triangle \(OMA\) has hypotenuse \(1\), hence \(OM=\cos x\), \(AM=\sin x\) and \(AB=2\sin x\). The area is \(\tfrac12\cdot AB\cdot OM=\sin x\cos x\).

Same triangle, same area:

\[\tfrac12\sin 2x=\sin x\cos x\;\Longrightarrow\;\sin 2x=2\sin x\cos x\]

2The cosine of a doubled angle

Measure the same chord \(AB\) a second way. Rotate the figure so \(A=(1,0)\) and \(B=(\cos2x,\sin2x)\). The legs of the right triangle are \(1-\cos2x\) and \(\sin2x\):

\[AB^2=(1-\cos2x)^2+\sin^22x=2-2\cos2x .\]

Step 1 gave \(AB=2\sin x\), so \(4\sin^2x=2-2\cos2x\), and with \(\sin^2x=1-\cos^2x\):

\[\cos2x=2\cos^2x-1\;\Longrightarrow\;\cos\frac{x}{2}=\sqrt{\frac{1+\cos x}{2}}\]

3Halving the angle builds the nested radicals

The rays above halve the angle each time. A right isosceles triangle with hypotenuse \(1\) gives \(\cos\frac\pi4=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\). Put \(a_1=\sqrt2\) and \(a_{n+1}=\sqrt{2+a_n}\). Claim: \(\cos\frac{\pi}{2^{n+1}}=\frac{a_n}{2}\). It holds for \(n=1\), and if it holds for \(n\), the half-angle formula gives \[\cos\frac{\pi}{2^{n+2}}=\sqrt{\frac{1+a_n/2}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{2+a_n}{4}}=\frac{a_{n+1}}{2}.\]

4Telescoping with the double-angle identity

Apply \(\sin2x=2\sin x\cos x\) to \(\theta\), then to the sine it leaves behind, again and again. Amber marks the cosines that pile up.

\[\sin\theta=2\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,{\color{#ffc107}\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}}\]
\[=4\sin\tfrac{\theta}{4}\,{\color{#ffc107}\cos\tfrac{\theta}{4}\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}}\]
\[=8\sin\tfrac{\theta}{8}\,{\color{#ffc107}\cos\tfrac{\theta}{8}\cos\tfrac{\theta}{4}\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}}\]
\[=2^n\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2^n}\,{\color{#ffc107}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2^k}}\]
\[\Longrightarrow\;\prod_{k=1}^{n}\cos\frac{\theta}{2^k}=\frac{\sin\theta}{2^n\sin(\theta/2^n)}\]

5The limit sin t / t → 1, by squeezing areas

Step 4 needs \(2^n\sin(\theta/2^n)\) as the angle shrinks. For \(0\lt t\lt 90^\circ\) three regions nest inside each other: triangle \(OAP\) (amber), sector \(OAP\) (cream) and triangle \(OAT\) (dashed, cut by the tangent at \(A\)).

Their areas are \(\tfrac12\sin t\), \(\tfrac12 t\) (the sector is the fraction \(t/2\pi\) of a disc of area \(\pi\)) and \(\tfrac12\tan t\). So \[\tfrac12\sin t<\tfrac12t<\tfrac12\tan t\;\Longrightarrow\;\cos t<\frac{\sin t}{t}<1 .\] Since \(\sin t<t\), \(1-\cos t=2\sin^2\frac t2<\frac{t^2}{2}\), so \(\cos t\to1\) as \(t\to0\). Squeezed:

\[\lim_{t\to0}\frac{\sin t}{t}=1\]

6Vieta’s formula

Put \(\theta=\pi/2\), so \(\sin\theta=1\): \[\prod_{k=1}^{n}\cos\frac{\pi}{2^{k+1}}=\frac{1}{2^n\sin(\pi/2^{n+1})}.\] With \(t=\pi/2^{n+1}\to0\), step 5 gives \(2^n\sin t=\frac\pi2\cdot\frac{\sin t}{t}\to\frac\pi2\). The product tends to \(2/\pi\). Replace each cosine using step 3:

\[\frac{2}{\pi}=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}}{2}\cdots\]

7The same product, read as polygon areas

Drawn to scale. The slider stops at N = 16 because from N = 32 on the cream sliver MQ = 1 − h is thinner than a pixel. That is exactly why h → 1 and the ratio tends to 1.

An \(N\)-gon in the unit circle is \(N\) triangles \(OPP'\) with base \(c=2\sin\frac\pi N\) and height \(h=\cos\frac\pi N\), so each has area \(\frac12ch\). Pushing the side’s midpoint out to \(Q\) makes a kite whose perpendicular diagonals \(OQ=1\) and \(PP'=c\) give area \(\frac12c\cdot1\). Triangle to kite is \(h\), for all \(N\) pieces: \[\frac{A_N}{A_{2N}}=\cos\frac{\pi}{N}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{A_4}{A_{2^{n+2}}}=\cos\frac\pi4\cos\frac\pi8\cdots\cos\frac{\pi}{2^{n+1}} .\] The square has \(A_4=2\) and the polygons fill the disc, \(A\to\pi\). So \(\frac2\pi\) is the product, the same result as step 6. Algebra and geometry agree, because \(1/(2^n\sin(\pi/2^{n+1}))=2/A_{2^{n+2}}\).

Speed. The error is about \(\dfrac{\pi^3}{6\cdot4^{n+1}}\): each factor divides it by 4, gaining roughly 0.6 digits.

Viète, 1593: the first infinite product for π, 63 years before Wallis.
