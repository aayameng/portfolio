Sophomore’s Dream

A derivation of Bernoulli’s identity by power series, substitution and the Gamma integral

ABSTRACT

We show that the integral of the self-power function $x^{-x}$ over the unit interval equals a sum of self-powers of the positive integers. The proof expands $e^{-x\ln x}$ as a power series, integrates termwise, and evaluates each term with the substitutions $x=e^{-u}$ and $t=(k+1)u$. Each step comes with an interactive figure.

$$\int_{0}^{1}x^{-x}\,dx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-n}\tag{1}\label{eq:goal}$$

1 · Series for $x^{-x}$

Start from the exponential series, valid for every real $u$:

$$e^{u}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{k}}{k!}\tag{2}\label{eq:exp}$$

Since $x^{-x}=e^{-x\ln x}$, put $u=-x\ln x$ in \eqref{eq:exp}:

$$\begin{aligned}x^{-x}&=e^{-x\ln x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-x\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\\&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\,x^{k}(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\end{aligned}\tag{3}\label{eq:series}$$

2 · Termwise integration

Integrating \eqref{eq:series} term by term gives

$$\begin{aligned}\int_{0}^{1}x^{-x}\,dx&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\,I_k,\\I_k&:=\int_{0}^{1}x^{k}(\ln x)^{k}\,dx\end{aligned}\tag{4}\label{eq:termwise}$$

JUSTIFICATION On $(0,1]$ we have $u=-x\ln x\ge 0$, so every term $u^k/k!$ is nonnegative. Tonelli’s theorem therefore lets us swap sum and integral with no extra hypothesis.

3 · Substitution $x=e^{-u}$

Let $x=e^{-u}$. Then $\ln x=-u$ and $dx=-e^{-u}du$. As $x$ runs over $[0,1]$, $u$ runs from $\infty$ down to $0$, so

$$\begin{aligned}I_k&=\int_{\infty}^{0}\left(e^{-u}\right)^{k}(-u)^{k}\left(-e^{-u}\right)du\\&=(-1)^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{k}e^{-(k+1)u}\,du\end{aligned}\tag{5}\label{eq:sub}$$

4 · The Gamma integral

Let $t=(k+1)u$, so $u=\frac{t}{k+1}$ and $du=\frac{dt}{k+1}$. Then

$$\begin{aligned}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{k}e^{-(k+1)u}\,du&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{k+1}\right)^{k}e^{-t}\,\frac{dt}{k+1}\\&=\frac{1}{(k+1)^{k+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k}e^{-t}\,dt\\&=\frac{\Gamma(k+1)}{(k+1)^{k+1}}=\frac{k!}{(k+1)^{k+1}}\end{aligned}\tag{6}\label{eq:gamma}$$

Combining \eqref{eq:sub} and \eqref{eq:gamma}:

$$I_k=\frac{(-1)^{k}\,k!}{(k+1)^{k+1}}\tag{7}\label{eq:Ik}$$

5 · Assembling the result

Insert \eqref{eq:Ik} into \eqref{eq:termwise}. The factorials cancel and the signs combine to $(-1)^{2k}=1$:

$$\begin{aligned}\int_{0}^{1}x^{-x}\,dx&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\cdot\frac{(-1)^{k}\,k!}{(k+1)^{k+1}}\\&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{k+1}}\end{aligned}\tag{8}$$

Setting $n=k+1$ gives the claim:

$$\boxed{\int_{0}^{1}x^{-x}\,dx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-n}}\tag{9}$$

$\blacksquare$

6 · Numerical convergence

Because $n^{-n}$ decays faster than any geometric sequence, each added term gains more digits than the last.
